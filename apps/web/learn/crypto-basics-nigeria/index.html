Crypto basics for Nigerians

The vocabulary you need, without the hype.

Beginner7 min readUpdated 2026-08

On this page

  • Coins and tokens
  • Stablecoins
  • Wallets and keys
  • Custodial versus self custody
  • Networks
  • Confirmations

Coins and tokens#

A coin is native to its own blockchain: BTC on Bitcoin, ETH on Ethereum, SOL on Solana. A token is issued on top of an existing chain. USDT is a token, which is why it exists on many chains at once and why the network matters when you send it.

Stablecoins#

Tokens designed to hold a steady value against a currency, usually the dollar. USDT and USDC are the common ones. For most Nigerians they are the practical use of crypto: a way to hold dollars without a domiciliary account.

Wallets and keys#

A wallet holds the private key that proves ownership. The recovery phrase regenerates that key. Whoever holds the phrase holds the funds, which is why it is written on paper and never typed into a website.

Custodial versus self custody#

On an exchange, the exchange holds the key and you hold a promise. In your own wallet, you hold the key. Self custody removes counterparty risk and adds personal responsibility, because there is no password reset.

Networks#

The same asset can exist on several chains, with different fees and speeds. USDT on Tron is cheap and fast, USDT on Ethereum is expensive and well supported. Sending to the wrong one is the most expensive mistake in crypto.

Confirmations#

A transaction is broadcast, then included in a block, then buried under further blocks. More confirmations means more final. This is why a transfer can be visible but not yet spendable.

You do not need to understand consensus algorithms to use crypto safely. You need to understand keys, networks and irreversibility.
