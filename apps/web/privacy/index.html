Introduction

Nasfam Enterprises (CAC Registration No. 7791264) operates Nasfam Pay and is the data controller for the personal data described in this policy.

This policy explains what we collect, why we collect it, the legal basis we rely on, who we share it with, how long we keep it, and the rights you can exercise. It is written to meet our obligations under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the regulations and guidance issued under it.

We collect what we need to run a financial service safely and to meet obligations imposed on us by law. We do not sell personal data.

Questions, requests, and complaints: support@nasfampay.com.

1. Personal Data We Collect

1.1 Information you give us

  • • Registration details. Full name, email address, phone number, username, and the password and Transaction PIN you set. The PIN and password are stored in hashed form and are never readable by us or by anyone else.
  • • Verification details. Bank Verification Number, date of birth, residential address, a government issued identity document, and a photograph of your face used to compare against that document.
  • • Financial details. Bank account number and account name for payouts, and the Virtual Account assigned to you.
  • • Transaction instructions. Wallet Addresses you enter, Networks you select, amounts, phone numbers and meter or smartcard numbers for bill payments, and Telegram usernames for Telegram products.
  • • Correspondence. Messages, support tickets, complaint details, and any documents you send us.

1.2 Information we generate or observe

  • • Transaction records. Amounts, rates and fees applied, timestamps, status, blockchain transaction identifiers, and destination addresses.
  • • Device and connection data. IP address, device model, operating system, application version, browser type, language, and time zone.
  • • Usage data. Screens visited, features used, session duration, and interaction patterns within the Platform.
  • • Risk and security data. Authentication events, device changes, velocity and pattern indicators used to detect fraud, and the outcome of screening checks.
  • • Communications metadata. Delivery and open status of notifications we send you.

1.3 Information from third parties

  • • Identity verification providers, who confirm whether the details and documents you supply match authoritative records.
  • • Banks and payment partners, who tell us that a transfer has been received, the sending account name, and whether a payout succeeded.
  • • Sanctions, politically exposed person, and adverse media screening providers.
  • • Public blockchain data, which is inherently public and which we read to confirm that a transaction settled.
  • • Analytics providers, which report how the Platform is used.

1.4 Sensitive personal data

The facial image used for identity verification is biometric data and is treated as sensitive personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. We collect it only where verification requires it, use it only to confirm that you are the person shown on your identity document, apply additional safeguards to it, and delete the working copy once verification is complete.

We do not collect data about your health, religion, political opinions, trade union membership, or sexual orientation, and you should not send such information to us.

1.5 Children

The Platform is not offered to anyone under 18 and we do not knowingly collect data from a child. Where we learn that an account belongs to a child we close it and delete the associated data, except anything the law requires us to retain.

2. Why We Use Your Data and Our Legal Basis

2.1 Lawful bases

We rely on the following bases under section 25 of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023:

  • • Performance of a contract. To open your account, execute the transactions you instruct, deliver assets and services, credit deposits, pay out to your bank, and provide support.
  • • Legal obligation. To verify identity, screen against sanctions lists, monitor and report suspicious activity, keep records, respond to lawful requests, and comply with directions from a regulator or court.
  • • Legitimate interests. To prevent and detect fraud and financial crime, secure our systems and your account, recover funds that were misdirected, understand how the Platform is used so we can improve it, enforce our Terms, and establish or defend legal claims. We balance these interests against your rights and do not rely on this basis where your interests override ours.
  • • Consent. For optional marketing messages and non essential analytics. Where consent is the basis, you may withdraw it at any time and withdrawal does not affect processing carried out before you withdrew it.

2.2 What we do with it

  • • Provide the Services. Execute purchases, sales, swaps, deposits, bill payments, and Telegram orders; maintain your balance; deliver assets to the address you nominate.
  • • Verify identity and apply limits. Confirm who you are, set the limits appropriate to your verification tier, and satisfy customer due diligence obligations.
  • • Prevent fraud and financial crime. Monitor transactions and account behaviour, screen counterparties and destination addresses, investigate reports made to us, and contain accounts where there is a serious risk.
  • • Keep the Platform secure. Authenticate you, detect unauthorised access, and protect our systems.
  • • Communicate. Send transaction confirmations, delivery alerts, security notices, service updates, and responses to your enquiries.
  • • Meet legal duties. File reports required by law, retain records, and respond to lawful requests.
  • • Improve the Platform. Understand feature usage, diagnose faults, and measure performance.

2.3 Automated processing

We use automated systems to screen transactions and account behaviour for fraud and financial crime. Where a serious risk is detected, an automated control may restrict money movement out of an account pending review. Incoming value is generally not blocked, so funds are preserved while the matter is examined.

You have the right to ask for human review of a decision made by automated means that significantly affects you, to express your point of view, and to contest the decision. Write to support@nasfampay.com and a person will review it.

We do not use automated processing to profile you for advertising.

2.4 Marketing

We send service messages that are necessary to operate your account, such as transaction confirmations and security alerts, and you cannot opt out of these while your account is open.

Marketing messages are sent only where you have consented or where the law otherwise permits. You can stop marketing texts at any time: switch off Marketing texts in your profile settings, ask our support team, or text STOP to 2442 to use your mobile network's Do-Not-Disturb service. A choice made in your profile or through support applies before the next message is sent.

3. Who We Share Data With

3.1 Categories of recipient

We share personal data only where there is a reason to, and only what is necessary for that reason:

  • • Identity verification providers, to confirm your identity and validate your documents.
  • • Banks, payment processors, and virtual account providers, to receive your funds and pay you out.
  • • Digital asset infrastructure providers, including blockchain node and exchange partners, to broadcast and settle transactions.
  • • Bill and airtime vendors and the telecommunications and utility companies behind them, to fulfil the payment you requested.
  • • Cloud hosting, database, authentication, and messaging providers that run our infrastructure.
  • • Analytics and error monitoring providers that help us understand usage and diagnose faults.
  • • Professional advisers, including lawyers, auditors, and insurers, where they need it to advise us.
  • • Law enforcement, regulators, and courts, where the law requires or permits disclosure.
  • • A purchaser or successor, in the event of a merger, acquisition, or transfer of our business, subject to this policy continuing to apply.

3.2 Processors act on our instructions

Where a provider processes data on our behalf, they act on our documented instructions under a written contract requiring confidentiality, appropriate security, and deletion or return of the data when the engagement ends. They may not use your data for their own purposes.

3.3 Blockchain data is public

A transaction settled on a public blockchain is recorded on that blockchain permanently, including the destination address, amount, and time. This record is public, is outside our control, and cannot be edited or deleted by us or by you. Do not treat a public address as private information.

3.4 We do not sell data

We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it with advertisers or data brokers for their own marketing.

4. Transfers Outside Nigeria

Some of the providers we use operate infrastructure outside Nigeria, so your data may be transferred to and processed in another country.

Where we transfer personal data outside Nigeria we do so in accordance with Part IX of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. We rely on one of the following: the destination country provides an adequate level of protection; the recipient is bound by contractual terms requiring protection equivalent to that required in Nigeria; the transfer is necessary to perform our contract with you; or you have consented after being informed of the risk.

We assess each provider before engaging them and require appropriate safeguards in writing.

5. How Long We Keep Data

We keep personal data only as long as we need it, and then we delete or anonymise it.

  • • Account and transaction records, and the identity and due diligence records that support them, are kept for at least five years after our relationship with you ends, because anti-money laundering law requires it.
  • • Verification imagery, including the facial image used to confirm your identity, is deleted once verification is complete. The result of the check is retained as part of the due diligence record.
  • • Support correspondence is kept for up to three years after the matter closes.
  • • Device, usage, and analytics data is kept in identifiable form for up to two years, after which it is aggregated or deleted.
  • • Fraud and security records, including the record of an account restriction, are kept for as long as needed to protect the Platform and to establish or defend a legal claim.

Where the law requires a longer period, or where data is relevant to a live dispute, investigation, or legal claim, we keep it until that requirement ends.

6. How We Protect Data

6.1 Technical and organisational measures

  • • Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  • • Passwords and Transaction PINs are stored hashed and are never held in a form anyone can read.
  • • Sensitive identifiers such as the Bank Verification Number are encrypted, and only the last digits are displayed where display is necessary.
  • • Access to production data is restricted to staff who need it, and administrative actions that move money require a separate PIN and are logged.
  • • Requests to our systems are subject to application integrity checks, rate limiting, and monitoring.
  • • Transaction and wallet balances are reconciled continuously against independent records so that discrepancies are detected quickly.
  • • Staff are bound by confidentiality obligations and receive training appropriate to their role.

6.2 Your part

Use a strong and unique password, keep your Transaction PIN private, keep your device and its operating system updated, and sign out on shared devices. We will never ask you for your PIN, your password, or a one time code. Tell us immediately if you suspect unauthorised access.

6.3 If something goes wrong

No system is perfectly secure. If a personal data breach occurs and it is likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will report it to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, as the Act requires, and we will tell you directly and without undue delay where the risk to you is high. Our notice will describe what happened, what data was involved, what we are doing about it, and what you should do.

7. Your Rights

7.1 What you can ask for

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 you have the right to:

  • • Be informed about how your data is used, which is the purpose of this policy.
  • • Access the personal data we hold about you and receive a copy.
  • • Rectify data that is inaccurate or incomplete.
  • • Erase data where it is no longer necessary, where you withdraw consent and no other basis applies, or where it has been processed unlawfully.
  • • Restrict processing while an issue such as accuracy is being resolved.
  • • Object to processing based on our legitimate interests, and to object to direct marketing at any time.
  • • Portability, meaning you can receive certain data in a structured, commonly used, machine readable format, or ask us to transmit it to another controller where technically feasible.
  • • Withdraw consent at any time where consent is the basis for processing.
  • • Not be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that significantly affects you, without the right to human review.
  • • Lodge a complaint with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.

7.2 Limits on these rights

Some rights are qualified. We cannot delete records that anti-money laundering law requires us to keep, we cannot remove a transaction already recorded on a public blockchain, and we may decline a request that would prejudice the prevention or detection of crime or the rights of another person. Where we decline a request we will tell you why, unless the law prevents us from doing so.

7.3 How to exercise them

Email support@nasfampay.com from the address registered to your account and describe what you want. We may ask you to verify your identity before we act, so that we do not disclose your data to someone else.

We respond within 30 days. Where a request is complex we may extend that period and will tell you if we do. There is no charge, unless a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive, in which case we may charge a reasonable fee or decline it and explain why.

8. Cookies and Analytics

We use cookies and similar technologies to keep you signed in, remember your preferences, secure your session, and understand how the Platform is used.

  • • Strictly necessary cookies enable authentication, session security, and fraud prevention. The Platform does not function without them.
  • • Analytics and performance technologies tell us which features are used, how long sessions last, and where errors occur. Some of these tools record interaction patterns within the Platform so we can diagnose usability problems.

You can block or delete cookies in your browser settings, but blocking strictly necessary cookies will prevent you from signing in. Where analytics is based on your consent, you may withdraw it at any time.

9. Changes and Contact

9.1 Changes to this policy

We update this policy when our practices change or when the law requires it. The effective date at the top of this page shows when the current version took effect. Where a change materially affects how we use your data, we will tell you through the Platform or by email before it takes effect.
